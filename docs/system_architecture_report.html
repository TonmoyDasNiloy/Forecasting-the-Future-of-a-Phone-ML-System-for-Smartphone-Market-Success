1. System Overview

This document describes the complete technical architecture of the AI-Powered Business Intelligence (BI) Agent — a smartphone launch intelligence platform. The system accepts a single structured input (the technical specifications of a new, unlaunched smartphone) and returns a comprehensive business intelligence dashboard covering:

  • Market positioning — predicted market segment (budget / mid-range / premium / flagship) with confidence percentage
  • Competitor identification — the most spec-similar phones currently on the market in the same region
  • Demand forecasting — a 6-month monthly unit sales curve for the new phone
  • Customer demographics — predicted buyer age, income, RFM/loyalty, and gender distributions
  • Product-Market Fit (PMF) — failure risk probability and a three-tier launch recommendation
  • Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) — analytically computed per-buyer value
  • Competitor gap analysis — normalised radar chart comparison across 6 spec axes
  • Price optimisation — profit-maximising price sweep anchored to competitor market pricing
  • Live market news — real-time web search for competitor and product reviews
Central Design Principle — Leak-Free Prediction Every ML model is trained exclusively on data known before a phone ships (specs, price, brand). Post-launch signals (sales, social media mentions, NPS, returns) are legally available in the training data but are never used as features for cold-start predictors. This is enforced at the code level via an explicit KNOWN_AT_LAUNCH vs POST_LAUNCH feature contract in config.py.

3. Data Layer

3.1 Training Dataset

The training dataset is global_smartphone_bi_v4_final.csv. It contains 5,801 unique phone-region records observed across multiple months (totalling several tens of thousands of rows) and 65 columns spanning device specifications, post-launch performance metrics, and buyer demographic distributions.

3.2 Feature Contract (Leakage Firewall)

Defined in src/config.py, every column is classified into one of two groups. This separation is the primary anti-leakage mechanism in the system:

KNOWN_AT_LAUNCH price_usd, 5g_or_not, num_cores, processor_speed, battery_capacity, fast_charging, ram_capacity, internal_memory, screen_size, refresh_rate, num_rear_cameras, primary_camera_rear, primary_camera_front, resolution_height, resolution_width, avg_rating, brand_name, processor_brand, os Only these features may be input to cold-start predictors (Pillars 2 and 4)

3.3 Feature Engineering

Seven additional features are derived at both training and inference time inside config.add_engineered_features(). All are computable from launch-known specs only:

FeatureFormula
perf_scorenum_cores × processor_speed
cam_totalprimary_camera_rear + primary_camera_front
pixelsresolution_height × resolution_width
battery_per_inchbattery_capacity / screen_size
ram_x_memram_capacity × internal_memory
price_per_ramprice_usd / (ram_capacity + 1)
value_score(ram + battery/1000 + cam_total/10) / log(1 + price)

3.4 Train / Test Split Strategy

Group-Based Split All models use sklearn.model_selection.GroupShuffleSplit with groups defined as brand_name + "__" + model. This ensures a phone never appears in both the training and test sets, preventing the model from memorising a phone's identity and inflating its score. Standard random splitting would be a form of data leakage on a multi-row-per-phone dataset.

6-Month Demand Curve

At inference time, predict_curve() generates a forecast by calling the model 6 times, sweeping months_since_launch from 0 to 5. All other features stay constant. This produces a trajectory showing how demand is expected to evolve month over month after launch.

Honest Performance Ceiling R² = 0.854 represents the genuine information ceiling of what launch-known specs can explain about demand. Testing showed that adding further engineered features or larger trees did not improve the score. The remaining ~15% of variance is attributable to marketing spend, launch timing, celebrity endorsements, and luck — none of which are recoverable from specs alone. Reporting a higher number would require leaky post-launch features.

4.3 Pillar 3 — Customer Segmentation (src/pillar3_segmentation.py)

Direct regression from phone specs to buyer demographics produces R² ≈ 0 — specs do not directly explain who buys a phone. Pillar 3 instead uses a retrieval-based approach: find the 10 most spec-similar phones that have already launched in the same region, and average the actual historical buyer demographics of those phones.

PropertyDetail
AlgorithmKNN retrieval — reuses the pillar1_similarity.joblib engine (no new model file)
k neighbours10 (region-restricted; falls back to global if region is sparse)
Output blocksAge distribution (5 bands), Income distribution (5 bands), RFM/loyalty segments (5 bands), Gender distribution (3 bands)
NormalisationEach block renormalised to sum to 100% so output is a valid probability distribution
Income R²0.771 — similar phones in the same region share buyer income profiles strongly
Age R²0.671 — moderate; region-aware matching more than doubled this versus global matching (was 0.32)
RFM / Loyalty R²~0.00 — reported as not predictable from device specs
Why RFM is Not Predictable Loyalty and recency behaviour are customer-level traits, not device-level traits. Whether a buyer is a champion, at-risk, or hibernating depends on their prior purchase history and brand relationship — not the phone's camera megapixels or battery size. Three separate methods were tested; all returned near-zero R². This is reported as a genuine finding rather than fabricated.

4.4 Pillar 4 — Product-Market Fit & Failure Prediction (src/pillar4_pmf.py)

The training dataset contains no pre-existing success or failure column. Pillar 4 therefore proceeds in two stages: first constructing a transparent PMF label from post-launch outcomes, then training a classifier to predict that label from launch-known specs.

Stage 1 — PMF Label Construction

A composite PMF score is computed per phone by aggregating post-launch outcome metrics, each component min-max normalised across all phones in the dataset:

pmf_score = 0.25 × NPS_score
           + 0.20 × repeat_purchase_rate
           + 0.20 × recommendation_rate
           + 0.15 × (1 − return_rate)       ← inverted: fewer returns = better fit
           + 0.10 × (1 − warranty_claims)   ← inverted: fewer defects = better fit
           + 0.10 × sales_slope             ← is monthly demand trending up?

Weight rationale: advocacy and loyalty signals (NPS + repeat + recommend = 65%) are weighted highest because they indicate genuine product-market alignment. Quality signals (returns + warranty = 25%) come next. Sales momentum (10%) is last since it is partly driven by marketing, not fit.

Failure label: Phones in the bottom 25th percentile of pmf_score are labelled failure = 1. Using a quartile cutoff rather than a fixed absolute threshold keeps classes balanced (25% positive rate by construction) and makes the threshold data-relative rather than an arbitrary constant.

5. Agent Orchestrator (src/agent.py)

The analyze() function is the single entry point that chains all four pillars into one unified inference pass. It accepts a Python dictionary of phone specs and returns a structured JSON dashboard.

5.1 Pipeline Execution Flow

PhoneSpec dict + region string
  │
  ├── [P1a] Segment classifier     → predicted_segment, confidence_pct
  ├── [P1b] Similarity engine      → closest_competitors (top-3 names, price delta)
  │
  ├── [P3]  Demographic retrieval  → age / income / RFM / gender distributions
  │         (uses P1b engine, k=10 neighbours)
  │
  ├── [P2]  Demand curve           → 6 monthly unit forecasts + total_horizon_units
  │
  ├── [P4]  PMF classifier         → pmf_score, failure_risk_pct, recommendation
  │
  └── [CLV] Analytic computation   → clv_per_buyer_usd
  │
  └──► Unified JSON dashboard

5.2 CLV Computation (Analytical, Not Looked Up)

Customer Lifetime Value is computed live inside the agent from first principles. The dataset's pre-calculated customer_lifetime_value_usd column is never read.

net_price      = price_usd × (1 − return_rate / 100)
repeat_factor  = 1 + (repeat_rate / 100)
clv_per_buyer  = net_price × 0.18 × repeat_factor × 2.4

The 0.18 margin is a stated industry-typical gross margin assumption for consumer electronics. The 2.4 loyalty horizon reflects the average number of smartphone purchase cycles a satisfied buyer gives a brand. Both are documented assumptions, not fitted parameters.

6.3 Price Optimisation Logic

The recommended price is derived from competitor market data, not from the user's input price. This makes the recommendation stable — changing the input price does not shift the recommendation. 

market_price  = median price of the 5 nearest spec-similar competitors
spec_ratio    = clamp(your_specscore / avg_competitor_specscore, 0.8, 1.25)
fair_price    = market_price × spec_ratio

unit_cost     = fair_price × (1 − 0.40)           ← 40% gross margin assumption
demand(p)     = base_demand × max(0, 1 − 1.3 × (p − fair_price)/fair_price)
profit(p)     = (p − unit_cost) × demand(p)

recommended_price = argmax_p [ profit(p) × pmf_score(p) ]  over 14 price candidates

6.4 News Search Parallelism

The /api/news endpoint uses Python's concurrent.futures.ThreadPoolExecutor (max 8 workers) to run all DuckDuckGo queries in parallel, with a 15-second collective timeout. Results are deduplicated by URL and interleaved across the "Your Phone" and "Competitor" buckets using round-robin to ensure both are visible early in the article list.

12. Design Principles & Known Limitations

12.1 Core Design Principles

1. Honesty About Accuracy The system was built to refuse fake accuracy. Post-launch leaky features (e.g. social_media_mentions, which correlates 0.86 with sales) are explicitly blacklisted from cold-start models. Every metric is measured on a held-out test set split by phone, not row, ensuring no identity leakage.
2. Retrieval Over Direct Regression Where Appropriate For customer demographics, direct regression from specs to buyer profiles yields R² ≈ 0. The system instead retrieves demographics from historically matched phones — a more honest and more accurate approach. This principle also underlies competitor identification.
3. Constructed Labels Over Absent Targets When a target variable does not exist in the dataset (PMF/success), the system constructs it transparently from documented post-launch outcomes using a weighted formula, rather than fabricating an arbitrary label or claiming a pre-existing column exists.
4. Analytically Computed Outputs Over Lookup CLV and price optimisation recommendations are computed analytically at inference time from first principles with stated assumptions — not read from pre-calculated dataset columns. This makes the outputs interpretable and defensible.

12.2 Known Limitations

LimitationDetail
Demand ceiling at R² 0.85 Specs genuinely explain ~85% of demand variance. The remaining ~15% is attributable to marketing spend, launch timing, and competitive events that are unrecoverable from specs alone. This is a data-structural limitation, not a tuning failure.
RFM unpredictable from specs Loyalty behaviour is a customer-level trait independent of the device. RFM fields in the dashboard represent population priors, not phone-level predictions.
PMF label is a proxy The constructed PMF label approximates market fit from post-launch signals. If the business has a specific definition of success (e.g. revenue target exceeded, product survived 18 months), the formula in pillar4_pmf.build_labels() should be updated and the model retrained.
Similarity engine assumes catalogue overlap For a truly novel device (new form factor, unprecedented price-spec combination), the nearest neighbours may not be genuine comparators. Retrieval quality degrades when the new phone lies far from any existing reference point.
News requires internet access The /api/news endpoint depends on live DuckDuckGo search. Offline or restricted-network deployments will not return articles.
